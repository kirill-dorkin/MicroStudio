You do not need a perfect technical specification before you speak to a developer. A few practical pieces of information can make the first conversation much more productive.

## Start with one sentence

Describe what your business does and how customers usually get in touch. For example: “We repair phones locally and take appointments through WhatsApp.” This gives the designer a starting point for the most important content and calls to action.

## List your services or products

Write down your main offerings, the information customers ask about most often and any prices you are comfortable publishing. Put your highest-priority services first. A clear sequence of information often matters more than the number of sections.

## Gather practical contact information

Make sure you know which phone number, messenger account, location and operating hours should appear publicly. If you want visitors to send a prefilled message, decide which account receives those messages before launch.

## Use real photography where possible

Photos of your team, workspace, services or products can make a site more credible. A logo in SVG format is especially useful. If imagery is missing, flag it early so you can decide whether to organize a shoot or use appropriately licensed alternatives.

## Share a few examples

Two or three sites you like are plenty. A simple explanation such as “I like this navigation” or “this page is too busy” can communicate your preferences better than a collection of design buzzwords.

## Separate essentials from future improvements

Do you need a simple contact action on day one? A multilingual catalogue? Booking? Online payments? A blog? These features vary widely in effort, so prioritize what must work at launch and what can be added afterward.

## The takeaway

Start with an honest description of your business and the action you want visitors to take. The website structure and technical details can be defined together before development begins.
