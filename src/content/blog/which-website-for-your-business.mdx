A landing page, a company website and an online store solve different problems. Choosing between them is less about the newest technology and more about what a visitor needs to understand and do.

## When a single page is enough

A one-page website can work well for a focused offer: a service, an event, or a specific advertising campaign. Keep the essentials close to the top: what you offer, who it is for, how pricing works, where you serve customers and how to reach you. A short site can be effective when the offer itself is straightforward.

Avoid trying to squeeze your entire business history into the first screen. Answer the visitor’s immediate question and let them find more detail as they scroll.

## When you need a company website

If your business offers several services, serves different audiences or needs detailed descriptions and examples, separate pages are usually clearer. They help visitors find the relevant part of your business, while letting you explain each service in enough detail for search engines and people alike.

More pages are not automatically better. A page deserves to exist when it answers a distinct question, not merely because you want to add more URLs.

## When you need an online store

A store needs product categories, helpful product details, clear availability, a cart and checkout process, delivery information and return policies. For small catalogues, a simpler website with product listings and a direct enquiry button might be a sensible first release. Full checkout and inventory management can come later if demand justifies them.

## Five questions before you start

What should a visitor understand in the first few seconds? What is the primary action — a message, call, booking or purchase? Do you already have copy and photos? Will you need multiple languages or integrations? Who will keep the site up to date after launch?

The answers help you set a practical scope, avoiding both unnecessary complexity and expensive omissions.
